# Football Talent Platform

## Problem Discovery

Version 0.2 (Hypothesis Draft)

---

# Purpose

This document captures the current hypotheses about the problems experienced by both aspiring sports communication talent and organizations that recruit them.

These problems are based on early observation, industry discussions, and product assumptions.

They are **not yet validated facts**.

The primary objective of this document is to define what must be investigated through interviews, market research, and field validation before product decisions are finalized.

This document intentionally avoids proposing solutions.

---

# Context

Indonesia has one of the largest and most passionate football communities in the world.

Every week, millions of supporters consume football content across television, streaming platforms, YouTube, TikTok, Instagram, podcasts, community watch parties, and football discussion forums.

This enthusiasm has also created a growing number of independent football creators who produce match reactions, tactical analysis, commentary videos, podcasts, livestreams, and other football-related content.

At the same time, opportunities within Indonesia's professional sports media industry remain relatively limited and concentrated.

Initial observations suggest that aspiring commentators, presenters, and sports creators often struggle to understand how they can transition from independent creators into professional careers.

Likewise, organizations appear to have limited mechanisms for consistently discovering and evaluating emerging talent outside their existing professional networks.

These observations form the basis of the hypotheses described below.

---

# Platform Perspective

Football Talent Platform is designed as a two-sided platform.

Long-term success depends on solving meaningful problems for both:

**Supply Side**

Individuals seeking opportunities in sports communication.

**Demand Side**

Organizations seeking trusted sports communication talent.

If either side fails to receive sufficient value, the ecosystem cannot become sustainable.

# Problem Discovery — Supply Side

## P-01 — No Observable Career Pathway (Hypothesis)

Early observations suggest that many aspiring football creators understand how to publish content but do not understand how to transform that work into a professional career.

For example, a creator may consistently upload football commentary or tactical analysis on YouTube or TikTok for several years without knowing:

* what competencies are expected by the industry,
* how professional commentators are evaluated,
* who reviews emerging talent,
* or what practical steps lead toward professional opportunities.

Validation Questions

* How do aspiring commentators currently expect to enter the industry?
* What is their biggest uncertainty?
* What do they believe is the next step after building an online audience?

---

## P-02 — Ability Is Difficult to Separate from Popularity (Hypothesis)

Current creator platforms primarily reward audience engagement.

Initial observations suggest that professional communication ability is often judged through follower count, views, or viral content because objective evaluation is difficult.

This may create situations where:

* highly skilled creators remain largely invisible because they have small audiences,
* while creators with larger audiences receive greater attention regardless of communication quality.

Validation Questions

* Have participants experienced this directly?
* How do recruiters currently discover new creators?
* Which metrics are actually trusted?

---

## P-03 — Lack of Industry-Recognized Evaluation Standards (Hypothesis)

There appears to be no widely accepted evaluation framework for emerging football commentators or sports presenters.

Based on preliminary discussions, industry evaluation may involve factors such as:

* clarity of communication,
* football knowledge,
* storytelling ability,
* match awareness,
* vocal delivery,
* professionalism,
* audience engagement.

However, these assumptions require validation with broadcasters and experienced professionals.

Validation Questions

* Which competencies matter most?
* Which competencies are measurable?
* Which competencies differentiate professionals from hobbyists?

---

## P-04 — Continuous Development Is Difficult

Current learning opportunities appear fragmented.

Creators may participate in workshops, local competitions, online courses, or community events.

However, early observation suggests these experiences are rarely connected into one continuous development journey.

Validation Questions

* How do participants currently improve?
* What motivates them to continue learning?
* Why do many creators eventually stop?

---

## P-05 — Most Competitions End at Winner Selection

Many competitions appear to focus primarily on selecting winners.

Participants who do not win often receive limited feedback, no structured learning path, and few opportunities for continued engagement.

This observation requires validation across multiple existing competition formats.

Validation Questions

* What do former participants value most after competitions?
* Would constructive feedback be more valuable than additional prizes?

# Problem Discovery — Demand Side

## D-01 — Discovering Emerging Talent Is Time-Consuming (Hypothesis)

Organizations appear to rely on a combination of recommendations, personal networks, online searching, and previous collaborators when recruiting sports communication talent.

Early assumption:

Finding qualified emerging talent requires considerable manual effort because there is no centralized source of trusted candidates.

Validation Questions

* How long does a typical recruitment process take?
* Where do recruiters currently search?
* Which recruitment channels produce the best results?

---

## D-02 — Recruitment Relies Heavily on Trust Networks (Hypothesis)

Initial observation suggests that recruitment in Indonesia's sports media ecosystem often depends on trusted recommendations and existing professional relationships.

This approach reduces recruitment risk but may unintentionally limit opportunities for new talent.

Validation Questions

* How frequently are new commentators recruited outside existing networks?
* What factors create trust during recruitment?

---

## D-03 — Evaluating Unknown Talent Is Difficult

Organizations may hesitate to recruit creators they do not know because publicly available content provides limited information about consistency, professionalism, preparation, or long-term potential.

Follower count alone may not reduce recruitment risk.

Validation Questions

* What information would increase recruiter confidence?
* What evidence is currently missing?

---

## D-04 — No Shared Talent Repository

Early observation suggests there is no nationally recognized database containing structured information about emerging football communication talent.

Recruitment efforts may therefore begin from scratch each time.

Validation Questions

* How do organizations currently maintain talent databases?
* Would a shared repository provide meaningful value?

---

## D-05 — Recruitment Mistakes Can Be Costly

Organizations may avoid experimenting with unfamiliar talent because unsuccessful recruitment affects production quality, audience experience, and brand reputation.

As a result, established names may continue receiving opportunities while emerging talent receives relatively few.

This assumption requires validation with industry practitioners.

---

# Cross-Cutting Problem

## P/D-01 — Geographic Inequality

Professional opportunities in sports media appear to be concentrated in major metropolitan areas such as Jakarta and several provincial capitals.

Aspiring creators from other regions may face additional barriers including:

* limited professional networks,
* fewer mentoring opportunities,
* higher travel costs,
* reduced visibility,
* limited exposure to broadcasters.

Validation Questions

* How significant is geography during recruitment?
* Which barriers are most influential?
* Can remote evaluation reduce these barriers?

# Initial Evaluation Dimensions (Hypothesis)

Preliminary discussions suggest that industry evaluation may involve multiple dimensions rather than popularity alone.

Potential dimensions include:

* Communication clarity
* Football knowledge
* Match analysis
* Storytelling
* Voice quality
* Audience engagement
* Professionalism
* Adaptability
* Preparation
* Consistency

These dimensions are working hypotheses and require validation with experienced practitioners before becoming product requirements.

---

# Opportunity Gap

Current alternatives solve isolated parts of the journey.

Some provide exposure.

Others provide education.

Others provide recruitment.

Initial observation suggests there is no widely adopted system that connects discovery, evaluation, development, reputation, and industry access into one continuous pathway.

Whether this gap represents a meaningful market opportunity remains a hypothesis that requires validation.

# Research Hypotheses

## H1

At least 70% of aspiring football creators interviewed cannot clearly describe a realistic pathway from independent content creation to professional sports media employment.

---

## H2

Most organizations rely primarily on recommendations, existing relationships, or prior collaborators when recruiting sports communication talent.

---

## H3

Both creators and recruiters consider follower count to be an insufficient indicator of professional communication ability.

---

## H4

Participants value structured professional feedback enough that they would consider it one of the primary benefits of joining a talent development program, independent of prize incentives.

---

# Validation Strategy

The hypotheses above should be tested through:

* In-depth interviews with aspiring creators.
* Interviews with broadcasters, clubs, and event organizers.
* Observation of existing competitions.
* Review of recruitment practices.
* Analysis of creator communities.

Problem statements should be revised whenever new evidence contradicts current assumptions.

# Document Principle

This document intentionally describes problems rather than solutions.

Its purpose is to ensure that future product strategy is driven by validated user needs instead of product assumptions.

Every problem included in this document should ultimately be supported by observable evidence before becoming a permanent part of the Product Constitution.
