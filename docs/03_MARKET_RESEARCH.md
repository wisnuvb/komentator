## Market Research

Version 0.1 (Working Draft)

# Purpose

This document evaluates whether Football Talent Platform is addressing a sufficiently large and sustainable market.

Its objective is not to produce precise market size estimates, but to understand:

* whether a meaningful problem exists,
* who experiences it,
* how the market behaves today,
* what alternatives currently exist,
* and whether the opportunity justifies building a long-term product.

Many sections in this document contain working hypotheses that require validation through market research and industry interviews.

---

# Executive Summary

Initial observation suggests that the market opportunity is **not the football commentator profession itself**, but the broader ecosystem of sports communication talent.

If the product is positioned solely as a football commentator competition, the addressable market appears relatively small.

However, if positioned as a long-term talent development platform connecting creators with industry opportunities, the potential market becomes substantially larger.

The product therefore competes less with existing competitions and more with the absence of structured career infrastructure.

# Industry Trends

Several long-term trends appear favorable for this product.

## Trend 1

Creator Economy continues to expand.

More individuals are building personal brands through digital content rather than relying solely on traditional media careers.

---

## Trend 2

Sports content consumption increasingly occurs on digital platforms.

Football discussion has expanded beyond television into YouTube, TikTok, Instagram, podcasts, livestreams, and online communities.

---

## Trend 3

Audience expectations continue to evolve.

Viewers increasingly value authentic personalities, tactical insight, storytelling, and creator-driven content.

---

## Trend 4

Organizations increasingly seek creators rather than only traditional broadcasters.

Football clubs, brands, and sponsors now collaborate with creators for marketing and fan engagement.

---

## Trend 5

Remote participation has become more widely accepted.

Creators can produce content and participate nationally without living in major metropolitan areas.

# Indonesia Context

Several characteristics make Indonesia uniquely interesting.

## Strong Football Culture

Football generates one of the country's largest and most engaged fan communities.

Discussion extends well beyond match days into social media, podcasts, supporter communities, and creator ecosystems.

---

## Digital-First Audience

A significant portion of football conversations now occurs on digital platforms rather than traditional television alone.

Independent creators play an increasingly important role.

---

## Opportunity Concentration

Professional opportunities remain concentrated in several major cities.

Emerging creators outside these areas may face higher barriers to visibility and networking.

---

## Relationship-Based Recruitment

Initial observation suggests recruitment within sports media often relies on trusted relationships and recommendations.

The extent of this practice should be validated through interviews.

# Competitive Landscape

Football Talent Platform competes indirectly with multiple categories.

| Category             | Examples                           | Strength             | Limitation                          |
| -------------------- | ---------------------------------- | -------------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| Creator Platforms    | YouTube, TikTok                    | Massive audience     | No career pathway                   |
| Social Media         | Instagram, Facebook                | Visibility           | Popularity ≠ professional readiness |
| Competitions         | Local commentator competitions     | Initial exposure     | Usually one-off events              |
| Education            | Universities, broadcasting schools | Structured learning  | Limited industry connection         |
| Talent Agencies      | Talent management                  | Career support       | Focus on existing talent            |
| Football Communities | Supporter groups                   | Community engagement | Informal development                |

No current category appears to combine discovery, evaluation, development, reputation, and industry connection within a single ecosystem.

This observation remains a working hypothesis.

# Initial TAM / SAM / SOM Hypothesis

Rather than estimating market size solely by counting aspiring commentators, the product should consider the broader sports communication ecosystem.

### TAM

Individuals in Indonesia interested in creating football-related communication content.

Potentially includes:

* creators,
* aspiring presenters,
* livestream hosts,
* podcasters,
* students,
* broadcasters.

Precise size requires validation.

---

### SAM

Individuals actively seeking professional opportunities within sports communication.

Likely represents only a subset of the broader creator economy.

Further segmentation is required.

---

### SOM

Initial target market should focus on participants willing to join a national football talent program.

Market penetration assumptions should be determined after pilot events and user interviews.

---

# Market Gap

Current alternatives generally solve one part of the journey.

Examples:

* visibility,
* education,
* networking,
* recruitment.

Initial research suggests there is no widely recognized platform connecting these experiences into a continuous career pathway.

This represents the primary market hypothesis.

# Market Risks

Several risks require careful validation.

## Risk 1

The actual market for professional sports communication may be smaller than expected.

---

## Risk 2

Participants may value prize money significantly more than career development.

---

## Risk 3

Organizations may prefer existing recruitment methods rather than adopting new platforms.

---

## Risk 4

Creator platforms may already satisfy most participant needs.

---

## Risk 5

Demand for emerging talent may be insufficient to support long-term platform growth.

# Open Research Questions

The following questions remain unanswered.

## Supply Side

* How many aspiring football creators want professional careers?
* What motivates them more: recognition, learning, income, or community?
* What prevents them from pursuing the industry today?

---

## Demand Side

* How often do organizations recruit new sports communication talent?
* What skills are hardest to find?
* How much value would organizations place on standardized evaluation?

---

## Market

* How many national competitions currently exist?
* How many participants do they typically attract?
* What happens to participants after competitions end?
* Are similar talent platforms successful in other countries?

These questions should guide future research before product strategy is finalized.

---

# Document Principle

This document intentionally separates observations, assumptions, and hypotheses.

Market size, customer behavior, and competitive positioning should be continuously refined as new evidence becomes available.

The purpose of this document is not to prove that the product should exist, but to determine whether the market justifies building it.
